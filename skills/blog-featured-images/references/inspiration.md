**Borrow principles, never ornaments:** variety comes from layout, never from palette or type. A reference's details are copied exactly only when the user asks for that look.

## Product blogs studied after the batch-5 rejection (Oct 19 2026)

The user: "go to browser, look for good websites that has blog pages, and then design using our design language."
- **Clerk** (clerk.com/blog). Taken: a big black headline with one real UI component beside it (a success toast with a green check, "Development keys configured / No account required", or a dark terminal panel bleeding off the right edge), the logo top-left, a light card. It's flat UI on a light field, like ours: it confirmed the direction (one real component per image) rather than becoming a layout.
- **Mintlify** (mintlify.com/blog). Taken: flat data viz and selection UI. A ring gauge (donut) with ticks on a dark field; a headline over a skeleton panel with one highlighted row; a selection box with corner handles around a phrase (our focus already does this); a headline beside a card of coloured horizontal bars; one app-icon tile alone on cream. Became: the ring gauge was tried as `versus/rings` (score rings on a white card) and disapproved; `versus/scores` (big gradient scores, no chart) replaced it. The rest was already ours (`focus`, `ranked`, `logotype`).
- **Resend** (resend.com/blog). Rejected: 3D renders and serif type (our "Never": AI-looking 3D). Only the big centred figure with a small label echoes our stat and badge.
- **Attio, Vercel**: text-only cards, so nothing to take.
- The lesson: good blogs build their art from their own product UI and data, drawn flat. Ours is the same: white cards, rows, bars, rings, selection boxes, logos on tiles, never devices or props.

The user: "map all of our previous templates that you created. That is the design language you need to follow, but you can go to browser and do your research… we have multiple colors, black background color, blue gradient, and then like so many other. And for layout… you can try variations."

- **Linear** (linear.app/now). Taken: one small card centred on near-black, nothing else. One tight centred group (taste 22).
- **Clerk** (clerk.com/blog, again). One real UI component beside the headline: one object per image.

- **Dribbble** (search "blog cover comparison"). Rejected: the covers were low quality; nothing used.
- The lesson: the strong blogs and our approved set agree. One object, one strong field, one centred group. Our own map came first; the research only confirmed it.
